# Explaining policy decisions to customers

A policy reply has to be firm about the decision and generous with everything around it: the reason, the
alternatives, and the path if the customer's case is truly exceptional. Customers accept "no" far better when they
understand it and still leave with something useful.

## Structure

1. **Acknowledge** what they asked for and why it matters to them (one sentence).
2. **State the decision plainly** in the first paragraph. Don't make them hunt for it.
3. **Give the reason in human terms** (one or two sentences): why the rule exists, not just that it exists. Link the
   public policy.
4. **Offer what you can do**: alternatives, partial options, workarounds, timing.
5. **Explain the path** if their situation changes or is unusual (how to ask for review, what info helps).
6. **Close** with a clear next step.

Example (refund outside window):

> Hi Jonas,
>
> Thanks for explaining that the team moved to another tool in August and the renewal went through unnoticed.
>
> I can't refund the annual renewal from 2 July, as it's outside our 30-day refund window for annual plans
> (policy: <link>). The window exists because annual pricing is discounted on the basis of a year's commitment.
>
> Here's what I can do:
> - Switch the account to the free plan now so no further charges occur, keeping your data accessible.
> - Apply the unused months as credit if the team ever returns within the next 12 months.
>
> If something about the renewal was unclear on our side (for example, you didn't receive the reminder email), reply
> with the details and I'll take it to our billing lead for review.
>
> Would you like me to switch you to the free plan?

## Decision rules for the agent

- **Check eligibility facts first:** plan, dates, usage, region, prior exceptions. Quote dates and amounts exactly.
- **Apply the written policy.** If the policy is ambiguous for this case, don't improvise; ask the policy owner.
- **Never invent policies, exceptions or promises.** Don't hint "maybe next time" unless that's actually true.
- **Statutory rights override company policy.** Consumer protection laws (e.g. cooling-off periods for consumers in
  the EU/UK) can require refunds regardless of the policy. If the customer is a consumer in a regulated region or
  cites a law, route to the team that owns legal/billing compliance rather than refusing. This skill is not legal
  advice; a qualified person must confirm legal positions.
- **Exceptions:** follow the documented exception criteria (e.g. our error, duplicate charge, outage during the
  period, first-time small amount). Record the reason so exceptions stay consistent and auditable.
- **Consistency:** same facts, same answer, regardless of tone of the request or social-media threats. Escalate
  public threats to the lead; don't change the decision under pressure.

## Wording

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| "Per our policy, we are unable to..." | "I can't refund this because..." |
| "There's nothing I can do." | "I can't do X, but I can do Y." |
| "Unfortunately" three times | One plain "no", once |
| "You agreed to the terms of service." | "The annual plan's refund window is 30 days." |
| "As a one-time courtesy" (when no exception was made) | Only say this when you really make an exception |

## Common policy scenarios

| Scenario | Decision clarity | Typical alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Refund outside window | Decline, cite window and reason | Downgrade, credit, pause, cancel future renewal |
| Plan limit reached (seats, storage, API rate) | Explain the limit and what triggers it | Upgrade options with price, cleanup steps, temporary increase if allowed |
| Account suspended for policy violation | State the violated rule category, not internal detection details | Appeal path, data export if allowed |
| Feature not on their plan | Name the plan that includes it | Trial of higher plan, workaround |
| Chargeback / disputed payment | Explain account status implications neutrally | Resolution path once dispute closes |
| Data deletion request | Confirm what is deleted, what is retained and why (legal retention), and timing | Export before deletion |

## Self-check

- [ ] Is the decision in the first paragraph?
- [ ] Is the reason given in plain words with the policy linked?
- [ ] Is there at least one genuine alternative?
- [ ] Are dates, amounts and plan names correct for this account?
- [ ] Have I avoided promising future exceptions?
- [ ] Does this case need legal/billing compliance review (consumer law, regulated region, threat of legal action)?

## Pitfalls

- Hiding behind "policy" without a reason; it reads as arbitrary.
- Over-explaining internal enforcement detail (fraud signals, detection methods) in suspension replies.
- Granting an exception silently with no record, creating inconsistent precedent.
- Mixing the "no" and the alternatives so the customer can't tell what was decided.
