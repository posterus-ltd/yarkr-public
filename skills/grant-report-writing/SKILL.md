# Grant report writing

A funder report answers: did you do what you said, what changed, what did you learn, and how was the money spent? It is
also the start of the renewal ask. Honest reporting of shortfalls, with explanations and adjustments, builds more trust
than glossy reports that don't match the numbers.

## 1. Gather

- The **award letter / agreement**: reporting period, required template or questions, deliverables, special conditions.
- The **approved proposal**: logic model, outcome targets, timeline, budget.
- **Data for the period**: outputs (counts), outcome measures (pre/post, follow-up), participant demographics if
  required.
- **Financials**: budget vs actual by line for the period, from finance (not estimated).
- Stories and quotes with documented consent; photos with releases.
- Any approved changes (no-cost extension, budget reallocation, scope change) and their dates.

## 2. Results against targets

Build the core table first; the narrative explains it.

| Promised (proposal) | Target | Actual (period / to date) | % of target | Status | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Families enrolled | 120 | 94 | 78% | Behind | Launch delayed 6 weeks by site renovation; spring cohort expanded |
| Parents using ≥ 2 techniques at 3-mo follow-up | 75% | 81% (n=62) | — | Met | |

- Report the denominator (n) for every percentage and how the measure was collected.
- Distinguish outputs (activity counts) from outcomes (changes in people).
- Don't swap in different measures because the promised ones look weak; report the promised ones and add others if
  useful.

## 3. Explain variances

For any line ≥ ~10% off target (or the funder's threshold), give: **cause**, **what you did about it**, and **expected
effect going forward**. Be concrete ("staff vacancy from March to May; position filled 2 June; enrollment recovered to
32/month"). Overperformance also needs explanation if it affects the budget.

Budget variances: compare spending to budget per line; explain variances above the funder's threshold, and note whether
prior approval was required for reallocations (many funders, and US federal awards under 2 CFR 200.308, require prior
approval for certain changes). Finance must confirm the financial figures; the report should never present estimated
numbers as actuals.

## 4. Narrative structure (if no template)

1. **Summary** (one paragraph): the period, headline results, one key learning.
2. **Progress on activities** against the timeline.
3. **Outcomes** with the results table.
4. **Story**: one participant or partner story (consented, anonymised as needed, 100–200 words) that illustrates a
   reported outcome, not a substitute for data.
5. **Challenges and adaptations**, with what changed.
6. **Learning**: what the evidence suggests about the model; what you'd do differently.
7. **Financial report** summary and variance notes.
8. **Next period / sustainability**: plans, other funding secured, and (for a final report) what continues after the
   grant.

If the funder provides questions, answer each one under its own heading, in order, within any word limits.

## 5. Tone and rules

- Lead with results, not activities. Specific numbers over adjectives.
- Credit the funder accurately; use their preferred program name.
- Keep consistent with prior reports — if numbers were revised, say so.
- Protect privacy: no identifying details without consent; aggregate small cells (suppress groups under ~10).
- Submit on time; if late is unavoidable, recommend the user contact the program officer before the due date.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every promised outcome and deliverable appears, even if not met.
- [ ] Percentages have denominators; period is clear (this period vs cumulative).
- [ ] Financials come from finance records and reconcile with the narrative.
- [ ] Variances explained with cause, response and outlook.
- [ ] Required attachments (financial report, data tables, products) listed and included.

## Pitfalls

- Reporting only good news; program officers notice missing targets.
- Mixing cumulative and period figures in one table.
- Stories with no consent, or that reveal identities in small communities.
- Copying the proposal narrative instead of reporting what happened.
