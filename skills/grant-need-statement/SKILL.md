# Grant need statement

The need statement convinces a funder that a specific, documented problem affects specific people in a specific place,
that it matters to *this* funder, and that a gap exists which the applicant is positioned to fill. It is about the
community's need, not the organisation's need for money.

## 1. Gather before writing

- **Funder priorities**: the RFP's stated goals, priority populations and geography, and any required data or sections.
  Mirror their language where honest.
- **Target population**: who exactly (age, income, condition, location), and how many the program will reach.
- **Geography**: the service area at the level funders recognise (county, census tract, ZIP, school district).
- **Data** (most recent available, cited): see sources below. Prefer local over national; use national only for
  comparison.
- **Organisation evidence**: waitlists, intake data, referral counts, client surveys, quotes (with consent).
- **The gap**: what services exist, why they're insufficient (capacity, access, eligibility, language, cost).

### Common US data sources

| Topic | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Demographics, poverty, income, language, housing | U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey (data.census.gov) — note 1-year vs 5-year estimates |
| Health outcomes by county | County Health Rankings & Roadmaps; CDC PLACES (tract-level estimates) |
| Employment | Bureau of Labor Statistics (LAUS for local unemployment) |
| Education | State education department report cards; NCES |
| Food insecurity | Feeding America Map the Meal Gap |
| Local | Community health needs assessments (hospitals publish these), United Way ALICE reports, city/county plans |

Outside the US, use the national statistics office and local authority data equivalently. Never fabricate a statistic:
if a figure is needed and unavailable, insert `[DATA NEEDED: <what, source to check>]`.

## 2. Structure (typically 1–2 pages, or the RFP's limit)

1. **Opening**: the problem in one or two sentences, concrete and local. Optionally a brief, consented client story
   (3–4 sentences, anonymised) — never a lead-in that runs half a page.
2. **Scope with data**: 3–5 statistics, each with source and year, compared to state/national benchmarks to show
   disparity ("28% of children in Riverside County live below the poverty line vs 16% statewide (ACS 2023 5-year)").
3. **Who is affected**: the target population, its size, and the consequences for them (health, economic, educational).
4. **Root causes / contributing factors**: briefly, so the program logic follows.
5. **The gap**: existing services and why they don't meet the need, with evidence (waitlist of 140 families; nearest
   provider 40 miles away; no Spanish-language services).
6. **Why now / why you**: urgency (policy change, rising trend) and the applicant's track record and community trust.
7. **Bridge to the solution**: one sentence connecting the gap to the proposed program; the program itself is described
   in the next section.

## 3. Writing rules

- Specific numbers beat adjectives: replace "many", "growing", "dire" with figures.
- Cite every statistic inline or in footnotes, with year. Use the funder's preferred citation style if given.
- Use asset-framed, person-first language ("young people experiencing homelessness", not "the homeless"); avoid
  deficit-only portrayals of the community. Describe strengths alongside needs.
- Don't make the need so vast the program looks futile; scope it to what the program addresses.
- No circular reasoning: "There is a need because there is no program" — show the consequences of the gap instead.
- Keep the organisation's financial need out of this section (it belongs in sustainability/budget).
- Match terms with the rest of the proposal (same population definition, same numbers in the logic model and
  evaluation).

## 4. Self-check before handing over

- [ ] Does each claim have a source and year? Are figures the latest available?
- [ ] Is the geography consistent with the service area elsewhere in the proposal?
- [ ] Does it address the funder's priorities explicitly?
- [ ] Is the gap one this program actually closes?
- [ ] Is it within the page/character limit?
- [ ] Could a reviewer unfamiliar with the community understand it?

## Output

Deliver the draft section, followed by a "Sources" list and a list of every `[DATA NEEDED]` placeholder and any
assumption the user should verify.
